05 — Poster design

One product.
One big idea.

Three advertising posters, each built around a single visual idea — type and product working as one image. Shown here in context, with the thinking behind them.

Food & beverage · Promo poster

Roast & Co. — Iced Coffee

A launch poster for a café’s iced coffee. The word COFFEE is turned on its side and runs the full height of the poster, while the cup bursts out of a chocolate splash across the two-tone split.

Poster designTypographyPhoto compositing
Role
Concept, layout, compositing
Format
3:4 poster · print & digital
Type
Concept project
01 · The brief

Make a cold drink feel exciting.

An iced coffee poster has to sell refreshment in a second — from across a café or in a fast scroll — and still leave room for the brand, a short message and a clear call to action.

02 · The idea

Turn the headline into architecture.

Rotating COFFEE ninety degrees makes it a pillar the whole layout leans on. The small “iced” sits on top like a label, and the tilted cup breaks the panel edge so the splash feels like it is leaving the poster.

03 · The craft

Warm palette, one loud voice.

Dark roast and caramel browns come straight from the drink. Rounded white type with a hard black offset shadow carries the shout; a soft script handles the description; a rust pill button gives the eye a final stop.

Fragrance · Product poster

Nova Essence

“Define your presence.” A perfume poster split down the middle — sage on one side, oxblood on the other — with the lettering swapping colour as it crosses the line and the bottle standing exactly on the seam.

Poster designSocial media 4:5Product retouching
Role
Art direction, layout, retouching
Format
4:5 social post · poster
Type
Concept project
01 · The brief

Give a small attar bottle a luxury stage.

The product is a compact amber bottle with a busy label. The poster needed to make it feel premium and desirable without redesigning the product itself.

02 · The idea

Two moods, one bottle.

The split background sets fresh against deep, day against night. The bottle sits on the divide, lifted by a crown of red liquid, while ESSENCE runs vertically down both edges like pillars framing it.

03 · The craft

Details that reward a second look.

Saffron strands are threaded through the letters of NOVA to hint at the scent. The serif type inverts colour at the seam, including the tagline, so the split reads as intentional from headline to footer.

Watches · Advertising poster

Casio — It’s About Time

A typographic watch ad where the product becomes part of the headline. The dial sits inside the word TIME, and the champagne lettering is colour-matched to the watch face on a near-black ground.

Poster designTypographic conceptSocial media 4:5
Role
Concept, typography, layout
Format
4:5 social post · out-of-home
Type
Self-initiated concept
01 · The brief

Say “classic watch” with almost nothing.

A dress watch sells on restraint. The goal was an ad with one line, one product and no decoration — something that would still read from a distance on a street light box.

02 · The idea

Let the watch finish the word.

“It’s about time” works as both a pun and a promise. TIME is broken over two lines at poster scale, and the watch drops into the gap after TI — its strap overlapping the letters so type and product become one object.

03 · The craft

Borrow every colour from the product.

The champagne of the letters matches the ivory dial, the gold case is the only highlight, and the brown strap is the only warm accent. A wide sans for the brand name against a high-contrast serif keeps the hierarchy clear.
